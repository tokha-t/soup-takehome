# What surprised me and what still concerns me

What surprised me was that the NF4 artifact retained 98.7% of the answer-loss improvement over a precision-matched base and received SHIP from Soup, yet lost 10.5 points of greedy lenient match compared with the fp16 merge. The repaired post-hoc scorer found only 499 valid SQL outputs in 573 shipped-setting generations, below the 95% requirement. Repeating the export produced byte-identical weights and the same greedy outputs, so this was a reproducible limitation of the tested artifact. Reviewing that evidence reinforced why I required both a relative improvement check and an absolute usability threshold. My remaining concern is that SQL planning acceptance still does not prove that the query answers the question correctly: string matching has its own limitations, and the synthetic execution fixtures often produced empty results. A release would need a better execution-based evaluation and another test of the exact quantized package, including its prompt and decoder. The small general suites, one training seed, and one runtime also leave uncertainty.
